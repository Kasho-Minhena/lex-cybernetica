# PART V — CYBERNETIC INTEGRATION AND THE SUBSTRATE INTEGRATION INDEX

## Article 21 — Cybernetic Integration

1. Cybernetic Integration means hardware, software, synthetic tissue, prosthetic systems, neural interfaces, remote computation or comparable systems that are functionally integrated with a Person or materially mediate the Person's functions.
2. Mere proximity, ordinary use, ownership or wearability does not by itself establish Cybernetic Integration.
3. Classification MUST follow actual function, dependence and control.

## Article 22 — Purpose and limits of SII

1. The **Substrate Integration Index (SII)** is a technical governance classification of a defined Person-technology configuration.
2. SII describes integration, dependence, control, resilience and failure consequence.
3. SII MUST NOT measure or be represented as measuring intelligence, dignity, morality, employability, insurability, citizenship, social value or Personhood.
4. Every SII report MUST state this limitation.
5. SII MAY inform technical safeguards only to the extent that the relevant configuration evidence supports them.

## Article 23 — Configuration-specific assessment and reassessment

1. An SII classification applies to a defined configuration, version and assessment date; it does not permanently attach to the Person.
2. A material change in software authority, remote dependence, authentication, network architecture, ownership/service structure, fallback, control, Essential Functions or failure consequences that could alter the classification MUST trigger reassessment without undue delay and within a period proportionate to the risk. Where delay could materially affect an Essential Function, safety, continuity or a high-consequence legal decision, the assessor MUST issue an interim **SII-Pending** or provisional status if final reassessment cannot be completed before the earlier classification would become misleading.
3. A provider or controller that causes or knows of such a change MUST notify the affected Person or lawful assessor and MUST NOT continue to represent the earlier classification as current.
4. A provider MUST NOT covertly manipulate an SII outcome by changing configuration while continuing to represent an earlier classification as current.
5. Reassessment MUST be explainable, evidence-backed and reviewable.
6. Personality development, legal recognition, fork status or relationship change does not alter SII unless the technical configuration also changes.

## Article 24 — Five-tier SII structure

The five-tier working structure is:

1. **SII-1 — Limited / Non-material Integration:** no Essential Function is materially dependent on integrated technology, or technology remains limited/supportive with low functional mediation and no material external control.
2. **SII-2 — Functional Hybrid:** one or more functions are materially supported by technology, primarily local and under Person or clinician-local control; external dependence or Material Control is absent or limited and safe continuity is reasonably available.
3. **SII-3 — Networked / Externally Mediated Integration:** at least one Essential Function is materially affected by recurring network, remote policy, software authority or external Material Control, while an independently operable and tested fallback preserves defined Essential Functions.
4. **SII-4 — Substrate-Dependent Hybrid:** multiple Essential Functions or continuity-critical processes depend on a tightly integrated synthetic architecture not safely severable in ordinary operation; fallback may preserve life or continuity without preserving independent normal function.
5. **SII-5 — Fully Post-Biological / Synthetic-Substrate Continuity:** continued cognitive or identity process, if Personhood is separately recognised, requires no functioning biological substrate and depends on synthetic or distributed substrate.
6. SII-5 does not create Personhood.

## Article 25 — SII profile, uncertainty and decisive-path rules

1. An SII assessment MUST evaluate, where applicable: Functional Mediation, Network/Remote Dependence, Software Authority, Material Control Exposure, Fallback Assurance, Reversibility/Severability, Failure Consequence, Cascade/Combination, Security Exposure and composition metadata.
2. A life-, identity- or continuity-critical dependency MUST NOT be averaged away by many low-dependence devices.
3. Device counts MUST NOT be added mechanically to produce a higher tier.
4. Combined systems MAY justify a higher classification where they create a shared failure or control path not present individually.
5. Fallback credit MUST NOT be given without evidence that the fallback works for the relevant function, duration and failure envelope.
6. Remote telemetry alone does not create Material Control.
7. Where material evidence is missing, the outcome MUST be **SII-Pending** rather than a guessed tier.
8. Annex A forms the technical implementation baseline for this Part.
