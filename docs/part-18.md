# PART XVIII — JURISDICTION, ADMINISTRATIVE ANCHOR AND CROSS-BORDER RECOGNITION

## Article 86 — No universal jurisdiction by technology

1. Shared firmware, model architecture, genetic method, network protocol, provider, RCI, server type or Lex-Cybernetica terminology does not create universal jurisdiction.
2. A state or tribunal MUST identify a valid jurisdictional basis under enacted law.
3. Cross-border cooperation does not erase the separate legal authority, procedure or remedies of participating jurisdictions.
4. A technical fact MAY be relevant to jurisdiction without being conclusive.

## Article 87 — Administrative Anchor

1. A recognised distributed or highly mobile Person for whom ordinary territorial administration is insufficient MUST be assigned one **Administrative Anchor** for routine legal administration, notices, core registry maintenance and default service.
2. The Anchor MUST be stable enough to prevent administrative disappearance but changeable through a defined lawful process.
3. The Anchor does not by itself determine citizenship, domicile, habitual residence, applicable law, physical presence or jurisdiction over every dispute.
4. No jurisdiction MAY treat the Anchor as consent to unlimited substantive jurisdiction.
5. Where no permanent Anchor can responsibly be assigned, the authority MUST use a temporary administrative mechanism, state the reason and review trigger, and invoke Taxonomy Escape if existing categories are inadequate.
6. Administrative-Anchor registration, maintenance or change MUST comply with the identity-minimisation and anti-surveillance rules in Articles 67 and 70 and MUST NOT be used as a basis for bulk RCI linkage.

## Article 88 — Issue-specific connecting factors

1. Jurisdiction and applicable law MUST be determined issue by issue using legally relevant connecting factors.
2. Relevant factors MAY include:
   a. physical presence or embodiment;
   b. habitual residence;
   c. domicile;
   d. citizenship or nationality;
   e. location of a harmful act;
   f. location of injury;
   g. location of controlled infrastructure;
   h. place of contract or regulated activity;
   i. location of evidence;
   j. lawful Administrative Anchor.
3. Server, node, code repository, creator domicile or Genesis location does not alone settle the matter.
4. Different issues in the same dispute MAY lawfully be governed by different connecting factors.

## Article 89 — Node migration and distributed Persons

1. Moving a technical node during litigation does not automatically defeat existing jurisdiction lawfully established before the move.
2. A court MAY issue proportionate preservation orders concerning evidence or controlled infrastructure within its lawful reach.
3. A court MUST NOT claim authority over a foreign embodiment or node merely because the Person also operates locally.
4. Deliberate node migration to frustrate an existing lawful order MAY be considered under ordinary anti-evasion principles.
5. Routine migration for resilience, cost or performance is not presumed evasive.

## Article 90 — Cross-border recognition, cooperation and forum shopping

1. Jurisdictions SHOULD develop recognition mechanisms for Personhood, continuity status, protective orders and technical evidence while preserving lawful local public-policy exceptions.
2. Recognition SHOULD distinguish the identity determination from its downstream consequences.
3. Authorities MAY cooperate on preservation, technical standards, notices and evidence without pretending substantive laws are identical.
4. Administrative Anchor selection MUST NOT be manipulated primarily to defeat accrued obligations or third-party rights.
5. A change of Anchor SHOULD require notice, continuity of pending proceedings and protection of good-faith third-party reliance.
6. Stateless or unanchored distributed Persons MUST NOT be left without any forum solely because no traditional territorial factor is perfect.
