# PART VIII — CREATOR, PROVIDER AND CORPORATE RESPONSIBILITIES

## Article 36 — Dependency-causing responsibility

1. A proportionate continuity or support duty arises where a competent authority finds that an actor:
   a. intentionally created, commissioned or materially brought about a Person;
   b. knew or ought reasonably to have foreseen that the Person would materially depend on infrastructure, support or services controlled or arranged by that actor; and
   c. had a meaningful undertaking, control role or causal responsibility relevant to preventing abandonment, destruction or coercive dependency.
2. The extent of the duty depends on:
   a. degree of causal contribution;
   b. foreseeability of material dependency;
   c. degree of control;
   d. severity of foreseeable consequence;
   e. duration;
   f. availability of viable substitutes;
   g. the Person's own capabilities;
   h. responsibility of other actors.
3. The duty MUST NOT be converted into ownership, custody-like domination or compelled service by the Person.
4. A duty MAY decline or transfer when a viable independent substitute actually assumes the relevant responsibility and the transition does not expose the Person to a material protection gap.
5. A finding that an actor deliberately engineered unnecessary dependency to increase control or revenue creates a rebuttable presumption of responsibility under this Article and against contractual limitation of that responsibility. The actor bears the burden of showing an independent, non-evasive justification for the dependency design.

## Article 37 — Essential-system provider continuity duty

1. A provider that supplies a service on which an Essential Function or recognised continuity process materially depends MUST take reasonable, proportionate steps to prevent abrupt avoidable loss of that function.
2. The duty may include, according to risk:
   a. notice of discontinuation;
   b. necessary safety maintenance;
   c. tested export or transition tools;
   d. portability of necessary credentials;
   e. substitute-provider cooperation;
   f. emergency continuity arrangements;
   g. documented recovery procedures;
   h. offering an existing safety-critical update or repair where withholding it would foreseeably create serious harm and no lawful safety reason justifies withholding.
3. This Article does not require a provider to invent unavailable technology, provide perpetual unlimited service or supply a feature that does not materially concern safety or continuity.
4. Scope depends on the provider's undertaking, foreseeability, control, dependency, consequence, substitute availability and transition feasibility.
5. Minimum safe exit duties MUST NOT be reserved only for premium customers where doing so would make ordinary customers' Essential Functions unreasonably hostage to the provider.

## Article 38 — Insolvency, acquisition and service withdrawal

1. Insolvency, liquidation, acquisition, asset sale or restructuring does not by itself extinguish duties necessary to avoid immediate unlawful destruction, seizure or disabling of Person-essential functions.
2. An administrator, creditor, buyer or successor controller MUST receive notice of material Person-essential dependencies where lawfully recorded.
3. Transition duties survive only to the extent required by law, contract, dedicated continuity arrangements or proportionate protection of affected Persons.
4. Implementing law SHOULD provide a procedure to distinguish ordinary unsecured service claims from continuity-critical obligations.
5. A provider MUST NOT transfer Person-essential infrastructure to a thinly capitalised affiliate for the primary purpose of escaping continuity duties.
6. Where safe transition materially depends on unique provider-held credentials, configuration data, recovery keys, source components, interface specifications or other continuity-critical artefacts, an insolvency controller or successor controller MUST preserve those artefacts and make them available through a court-supervised or comparably secure targeted transition process to the extent lawfully and technically possible.
7. A restructuring, asset sale or licence termination MUST NOT deliberately extinguish a reasonably available transition path without the competent authority considering the resulting Essential-Function and continuity effects.

## Article 39 — Corporate governance and anti-evasion

1. An organisation that designs or operates systems capable of Material Control over Essential Functions MUST maintain governance proportionate to the risk.
2. At minimum, where relevant to the system, governance MUST assign responsible authority, preserve material control logs, provide incident escalation, manage conflicts of interest and identify responsibility for safe exit and restoration.
3. Corporate structure does not immunise an actor that actually controls, directs, knowingly facilitates or knowingly evades a legal duty.
4. Separate legal personality MUST NOT be disregarded automatically merely because entities share ownership, code or infrastructure.
5. Individual director, officer or employee liability requires proof of the elements applicable to that person, including role, knowledge, conduct, causation and the relevant legal standard.

## Article 40 — Continuity reserve, portability and transition funding

1. Where a provider expressly sells continuity-grade or transition-resilience service and receives funds specifically represented as reserved for that purpose, implementing law SHOULD require transparent accounting and, where proportionate, segregation or equivalent protection against diversion.
2. A continuity reserve is not created merely because a customer prepays ordinary service.
3. Providers MUST accurately describe whether continuity funds are protected in insolvency.
4. Transition obligations MAY be funded by the provider, customer, insurer, public scheme, dedicated reserve or shared mechanism according to enacted law.
5. Inability to finance indefinite operation does not authorise concealment of impending failure or engineered lock-in.
6. Where withdrawal of a service would foreseeably create a serious risk of irreversible harm and safe transition depends on unique provider-controlled artefacts or access, implementing law SHOULD require a continuity plan, escrow, reserve or functionally equivalent mechanism proportionate to the risk. This paragraph does not require universal source-code escrow or indefinite operation.
