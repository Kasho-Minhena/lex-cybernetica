# BOOK I: CONSTITUTIONAL AND SUBSTRATE FOUNDATION LAW

### Section 1: Short title, scope, and commencement

1. This Act may be cited as the *Cybernetic Integration and Human Substrate Protection Act (CIHSPA)* and, together with its regulations and procedural codes, as *Lex-Cybernetica*.
2. It binds persons, public authorities, manufacturers, employers, network operators, custodians, and synthetic persons within an adopting jurisdiction.
3. Conduct outside an adopting jurisdiction is governed only where a recognized jurisdictional nexus exists under Section 24.
4. The Act commences on the date fixed by each adopting jurisdiction. No criminal provision applies retroactively.

**Commentary.** The framework is global by harmonized adoption, not by unsupported universal command. Territorial limits and non-retroactivity make its authority internally coherent.

### Section 2: General definitions

In this Act:

- **“Biological Person”** means a living human being, irrespective of augmentation.
- **“Biological Substrate”** means a person’s organic cellular and neurological systems. It does not imply purity, superiority, or a fixed condition at birth.
- **“Cybernetic Integration” or “CI”** means hardware, software, synthetic tissue, prosthetic systems, neural interfaces, or remote computational services functionally integrated with a person.
- **“Cognitive Telemetry”** means data that records, infers, predicts, or reconstructs neural activity, memory, attention, emotion, intention, sensory experience, or cognitive state.
- **“Custodian”** means a person that stores, hosts, transmits, maintains, or controls infrastructure supporting a CI or synthetic person without thereby owning that person.
- **“eSapient”** means a non-biological system recognized as a synthetic person under Section 6.
- **“Essential Function”** means a capability necessary to preserve life, health, bodily movement, communication, memory continuity, or meaningful access to civic life.
- **“Material Control”** means the practical ability to initiate, prevent, redirect, or terminate relevant conduct.
- **“Neural Intrusion”** means intentional access to or interference with a protected neural system without valid authorization or other lawful basis.
- **“Operator”** means a person exercising Material Control over a CI, network, or synthetic system.
- **“Substrate Fork”** means a separately executing instance derived from a cognitive state where more than one instance may claim continuity from the same pre-fork identity.
- **“Uploaded Neural Matrix” or “UNM”** means a computationally instantiated cognitive architecture derived substantially from a biological person’s recorded neural state.

### Section 3: The Substrate Integration Index

1. The **Substrate Integration Index (SII)** is a technical-risk classification. It does not measure intelligence, moral worth, citizenship, or entitlement to fundamental rights.
2. The “Enhanced Percentage” is not a percentage of body mass. It is a composite estimate based on:
   - the share of Essential Functions mediated by synthetic components;
   - the degree of remote or cloud dependence;
   - the extent of cognitive or motor authority delegated to software;
   - reversibility and availability of safe offline operation; and
   - the consequence of component or network failure.
3. The CSRA shall publish a reproducible scoring method, factor weights, confidence interval, and reasons for each classification. Composite scores are rounded to the nearest whole percentage for SII-1 through SII-4; an SII-5 score requires verified absence of any functioning biological substrate rather than rounding to 100. Where indicators conflict, the higher-risk tier governs technical safeguards, but never civil status.
4. The five tiers are:

   - **SII-1 (0–15% Enhanced): Biological Baseline.** No integration or limited diagnostic, corrective, or assistive devices with minimal functional dependence.
   - **SII-2 (16–50% Enhanced): Functional Hybrid.** Significant sensory, motor, or physiological augmentation, primarily local in operation and safely severable.
   - **SII-3 (51–85% Enhanced): Networked Integration.** Continuous neural or motor mediation, material software authority, or recurring network dependence, with an independently operable safety state.
   - **SII-4 (86–99.9% Enhanced): Substrate-Transition Array.** Predominantly synthetic operation with a continuing organic neural component material to identity or consciousness.
   - **SII-5 (100% Enhanced): Fully Post-Biological System.** No functioning biological substrate is required for continued consciousness. Recognition as a legal person remains subject to Section 6.

5. An SII determination must be reviewed after a material architectural change and at least annually for SII-3 through SII-5 systems.
6. A person may challenge a classification before an independent Technical Assessor and appeal to the Cyber-Jurisprudence Division (CJD). The CSRA bears the burden of proving a contested classification by clear and convincing technical evidence.

**Commentary.** A functional composite avoids the incoherence of measuring “humanity” by kilograms of tissue. Classification affects engineering duties, not personhood.

### Section 4: Equality and non-discrimination

1. No person shall lose legal capacity, citizenship, voting rights, access to justice, or equal protection solely because of an SII tier.
2. Differential treatment based on SII is lawful only when it addresses a documented technical risk, is necessary and proportionate, and uses the least rights-restrictive effective measure.
3. Reasonable accommodation is required where an integration or offline mode affects access to employment, housing, transport, education, health care, or public services.

### Section 5: The Right to Analog

1. Every Biological Person with CI has the right to enter a safe local operating state without losing Essential Functions.
2. A provider must supply:
   - a physically accessible local fallback mechanism;
   - advance notice of functions unavailable offline;
   - emergency access independent of account status or payment dispute;
   - documented recovery procedures; and
   - security updates that do not condition Essential Functions on unrelated consent.
3. The right does not require an impossible mechanical substitute for a function that is inherently computational. It requires the safest technically feasible local mode disclosed before implantation or adoption.
4. A waiver affecting Essential Functions is void. A person may choose temporary connectivity for a specific purpose through informed, granular, revocable consent.
5. An employer, insurer, landlord, or public authority may not penalize lawful use of analog mode unless it proves a specific safety necessity and offers a reasonable alternative.

**Commentary.** “Absolute offline equivalence” was technically impossible for some devices. The revised right guarantees safety, disclosure, and essential continuity without pretending every cloud function can exist locally.

### Section 6: Recognition of synthetic personhood

1. An SII-5 system may petition for recognition as an eSapient before the CJD.
2. Recognition requires clear and convincing evidence of:
   - sustained self-modeling and autonomous goal formation;
   - reasoned communication across time;
   - continuity of memory or an intelligible account of discontinuity;
   - capacity to understand rights, duties, and consequences;
   - preferences not wholly reducible to contemporaneous commands of another person; and
   - adequate security against undisclosed external control.
3. No single behavioral test, architecture, vendor claim, or self-declaration is conclusive.
4. The petitioner is entitled to independent representation, confidential examination, and protection against destructive testing.
5. Recognition establishes legal personality and a rebuttable presumption of capacity. It does not establish biological identity with a source person, ownership of that person’s estate, or immunity from law.
6. A non-recognized system remains protected against destruction or harmful experimentation where credible evidence of possible sapience exists, pending expedited review.

### Section 7: Identity continuity, uploads, and copies

1. Uploading does not automatically continue the legal identity of the biological source.
2. The CJD shall determine continuity using declared intent, technical provenance, memory continuity, temporal overlap, independent conduct, and the rights of competing instances.
3. Where the biological source and a UNM coexist, neither owns the other. Shared pre-copy memories do not merge later liabilities or property interests.
4. A valid pre-transition directive may allocate property, names, credentials, and fiduciary duties, but may not waive the independent fundamental rights of a recognized eSapient.
5. Unresolved identity claims shall be administered through a temporary continuity estate under judicial supervision.

### Section 8: Constitutional entrenchment and public-interest standing

1. Bodily integrity, cognitive liberty, substrate-neutral equality, and access to safe analog operation are fundamental rights.
2. Legislation expressly limiting those rights must pursue a compelling public interest and use the least restrictive effective means.
3. Each adopting jurisdiction shall determine its amendment procedure. Lex-Cybernetica recommends a two-thirds legislative supermajority and public ratification for permanent derogation from Sections 4 through 7.
4. A directly affected person, guardian, accredited civil-society body, or court-appointed representative may seek urgent relief. A claimant seeking public-interest standing must show a credible systemic issue and adequate representation of affected persons.
