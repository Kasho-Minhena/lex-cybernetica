Research

A framework built to expose hard questions, not hide them.

Lex-Cybernetica treats future law as a research problem spanning doctrine, philosophy, technology, medicine, governance and social institutions.

Research line 01

Personhood without substrate essentialism

What facts should matter when law recognises a Person whose embodiment, cognition or continuity may be distributed across biological and technical systems?

Research line 02

Continuity, forks and successor persons

When does restoration preserve identity, when does replication create another Person, and when does a successor relationship arise?

Research line 03

Control without ownership of persons

How should law distinguish ownership of components and intellectual property from impermissible ownership or domination of a recognised Person?

Research line 04

Institutions for uncertain futures

Which courts, registries, review bodies, evidentiary processes and technical experts can make future-law decisions contestable and explainable?

Research line 05

Local functional autonomy and dependence

How should law respond when Essential Functions depend on remote providers, networks, permissions, subscriptions or infrastructure?

Research line 06

Cross-border continuity and recognition

How should identity, Personhood and continuity claims travel across jurisdictions without creating legal voids or duplication?

Research method

Doctrine → challenge → scenario test → controlled revision.

Version 3.0 preserves provenance and separates research evidence from Canon. Scenario work may expose weaknesses, but no outside comment, AI review or fictional outcome changes the framework automatically.

  1. 1Frame the legal problem.
  2. 2Separate concepts that should not be mechanically linked.
  3. 3Draft or identify the applicable doctrine.
  4. 4Stress-test with cases, science, technical evidence and comparative law.
  5. 5Record dissent, failure modes and provenance.
  6. 6Route substantive change through explicit version control.
Research / Canon boundary

The Founder-Frozen Publication Edition remains fixed. Later substantive change requires a separately controlled amendment or successor edition.
