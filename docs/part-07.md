# PART VII — CONSENT, SUPERVISION AND EMERGENCY CONTROL

## Article 31 — Valid consent

1. Consent to cybernetic, cognitive, Genesis, medical or Material-Control activity is valid only where the consenting Person has relevant capacity, receives material information in an understandable form, acts voluntarily, and consents to a sufficiently specific purpose and scope.
2. Consent to one function or service does not automatically authorise materially different control.
3. Bundled consent is invalid to the extent that refusal of an unrelated invasive function is made impossible without genuine necessity.
4. A material economic, employment, care or infrastructure dependency MUST be considered when assessing voluntariness.
5. The actor relying on consent bears the burden of showing its validity where the consent authorises Material Control or highly sensitive cognitive access.

## Article 32 — Withdrawal, material change and irreversible action

1. A Person MAY withdraw consent prospectively, subject to obligations already lawfully accrued and to narrow emergency rules.
2. A material change in software authority, control, data use, cognitive access, remote dependence or Essential Function risk requires fresh disclosure and, where the activity is consent-based, fresh consent.
3. Where withdrawal cannot immediately restore a prior state, the provider MUST offer the safest reasonably available transition and explain irreversible consequences.
4. A contract MUST NOT treat technical lock-in deliberately created by the provider as proof that withdrawal is impossible.

## Article 33 — State supervision

1. State licensing, regulation, custody, employment, public safety responsibility or supervision of an activity does not by itself authorise continuing control over a Person's body, cognition or Essential Functions.
2. State Material Control requires a lawful basis, legitimate purpose, necessity, proportionality and review.
3. A Person affected by non-emergency state control IS ENTITLED TO reasons, notice, an opportunity to be heard and meaningful review.
4. Secret or ex parte control MAY be used only where authorised by law and strictly necessary; it MUST be subject to prompt independent review.

## Article 34 — Emergency entry into control

1. Emergency Material Control MAY be exercised only where:
   a. there is an imminent or rapidly developing risk of serious harm;
   b. the actor has lawful authority or a recognised emergency duty;
   c. the intervention is reasonably necessary;
   d. no less intrusive effective measure is reasonably available in time.
2. The scope of control MUST be limited to the functions required to address the emergency.
3. The emergency basis MUST be logged contemporaneously where technically feasible.
4. Lawful emergency entry does not create continuing authority after necessity ends.

## Article 35 — Termination, restoration and after-action review

1. Emergency authority MUST terminate when the factual necessity ends.
2. Continuing Material Control after that point is a separate act requiring its own lawful basis.
3. The controlling actor MUST restore ordinary authority as soon as safely practicable.
4. A material emergency intervention MUST generate an after-action record containing trigger, scope, duration, commands, data access, exit condition and restoration steps.
5. High-consequence interventions MUST be independently reviewable within a short period fixed by implementing law.
6. Harm caused by unlawful continuation is independently remediable even if the initial emergency intervention was lawful.
