# VOLUME III: CYBER-CIVIL PROCEDURE AND NEURAL EVIDENCE CODE

## Chapter 1: The Cyber-Jurisprudence Division

- **Rule 1.1 — Composition:** CJD cases are decided by independent judges. Technical Assessors advise on technical questions but do not determine ultimate legal liability.
- **Rule 1.2 — Reasons and review:** Decisions must identify findings, evidentiary confidence, legal rules, and remedies. Final decisions are appealable to the adopting jurisdiction’s ordinary appellate system.
- **Rule 1.3 — Capacity and representation:** A court must provide suitable representation for a person whose capacity, continuity, or sapience is genuinely disputed.

## Chapter 2: Pleading, emergency relief, and preservation

- **Rule 2.1 — Pleading:** A claimant must state the relevant actor, system, SII tier if known, material firmware or model version, alleged control path, harm, causal theory, and relief. Unknown technical facts may be pleaded on information and belief where the defendant controls them.
- **Rule 2.2 — Substrate Preservation Order (SPO):** A court may order narrowly tailored preservation on a showing that relevant evidence is likely to be lost. The order must specify scope, duration, custodian, security, access, and procedure for privileged material.
- **Rule 2.3 — Ex parte relief:** Relief without notice requires specific evidence of imminent loss or harm, expires within 72 hours unless renewed after a hearing, and may require security for wrongful restraint.
- **Rule 2.4 — No compelled consciousness:** Preservation ordinarily targets evidence, logs, and snapshots. A court may not compel continued conscious execution unless strictly necessary to prevent imminent extinction and no less intrusive alternative exists.

## Chapter 3: Secure discovery

- **Rule 3.1 — Proportionality:** Discovery must be relevant, proportionate, and designed to minimize exposure of cognitive content, security secrets, and unrelated personal data.
- **Rule 3.2 — Secure Virtual Data Room (SVDR):** Sensitive code may be reviewed in an access-controlled environment with logging, role-based permissions, and export restrictions established by protective order.
- **Rule 3.3 — Effective challenge:** A zero-download rule may not prevent meaningful testing. Courts may authorize sandbox execution, reproducible queries, independent tools, or sealed extracts where necessary.
- **Rule 3.4 — Provenance:** Evidence handling must record cryptographic hashes, collection method, transformations, access, and tool versions. Cryptographic integrity proves consistency, not truth or proper interpretation.

## Chapter 4: Neural Evidence Code

- **Rule 4.1 — Admissibility:** Neural evidence is admissible only if relevant and if its proponent establishes authentic source, reliable acquisition, validated interpretation, known error characteristics, and an intact chain of custody.
- **Rule 4.2 — Weight:** Courts must distinguish raw signal, processed feature, probabilistic inference, reconstructed memory, and expert interpretation. An inference may not be described as direct observation.
- **Rule 4.3 — Coercion:** Neural evidence obtained through torture, compelled alteration, or an unlawful intrusion is excluded from criminal prosecution of the affected person, subject only to a narrowly defined and independently sourced impeachment rule established by the adopting constitution.
- **Rule 4.4 — Alteration:** Evidence of alteration does not require categorical exclusion if the reliable portion can be separated and fairly evaluated. The court must identify uncertainty and guard against misleading precision.
- **Rule 4.5 — Self-incrimination:** Compelled disclosure of cognitive content receives the highest constitutional protection. A warrant for stored technical records does not automatically authorize interrogation of a mind.

## Chapter 5: Experts and cognitive privilege

- **Rule 5.1 — Expert reliability:** An expert must disclose qualifications, methods, validation, error rates where known, assumptions, conflicts, and limitations. The court must assess reliability rather than defer to certification alone.
- **Rule 5.2 — Cognitive privacy:** Uncommunicated thought is not discoverable merely because it is technically accessible.
- **Rule 5.3 — Client-Neural Privilege (CNP):** Confidential cognitive communications intentionally shared with legal counsel or a counsel-directed system for legal advice are privileged to the same extent as attorney-client communications.
- **Rule 5.4 — Limits:** CNP does not protect communications made to facilitate a future crime or fraud, operational commands independently relevant to ongoing harm, or content placed at issue by the holder, subject to narrow judicial review.
- **Rule 5.5 — Neutral assessors:** Court-appointed assessors owe duties to the court, must protect confidential information, and may be examined by the parties.
