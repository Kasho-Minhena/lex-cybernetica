# PART III — LEGAL CAPACITY AND SUPPORTED DECISION-MAKING

## Article 11 — Presumption of capacity

1. A recognised Person IS PRESUMED to have legal capacity.
2. Capacity MUST NOT be denied on the basis of disability, SII tier, dependence on support, synthetic origin, chronological age since activation, unfamiliar embodiment, communication method or distributed operation.
3. An actor challenging a Person's capacity bears the burden of showing a decision-specific reason for restriction.

## Article 12 — Decision-specific assessment

1. Capacity MUST be assessed in relation to the particular decision, its consequences, the available information and the support reasonably available.
2. The assessment MUST distinguish inability from unconventional reasoning, unfamiliar values, slow communication, supported communication, temporary network loss or disagreement with professional advice.
3. A finding concerning one decision MUST NOT create a general incapacity status unless separately justified.

## Article 13 — Support before restriction

1. Reasonable decision support MUST be attempted before legal authority is transferred away from a Person where support can materially improve participation.
2. Support MAY include communication assistance, translation, interface access, additional time, independent explanation, trusted-person assistance, temporary technical restoration or accessible presentation.
3. Support MUST NOT become covert substitution, surveillance or control.
4. A provider or guardian with a financial or technical conflict MUST disclose that conflict.

## Article 14 — Temporary safeguards for high-risk decisions

1. A competent authority MAY impose a temporary safeguard where objective evidence shows a substantial and specific risk of grave, irreversible harm and ordinary support is insufficient.
2. The safeguard MUST:
   a. target the particular decision;
   b. use the least restrictive effective measure;
   c. state its factual basis;
   d. expire automatically unless renewed on fresh evidence;
   e. provide prompt review.
3. Synthetic chronological age alone is insufficient.
4. Lack of lived experience may be relevant only where it is demonstrably connected to the decision risk.

## Article 15 — Fluctuating capacity

1. Capacity that varies with network, medication, power, interface, fatigue, system state or other condition MUST be assessed at the relevant time.
2. Where practical, a high-consequence decision SHOULD be deferred to a reasonably expected period of restored capacity rather than permanently transferred.
3. A transaction entered during an unexpected loss of essential decision support MUST be reviewable where the other party knew or ought reasonably to have known of the impairment and exploited it.
4. Emergency decisions remain governed by Articles 34 and 35.
5. Temporary loss of network-mediated assistance, temporary cognitive partition or temporary reduction in available support MUST NOT by itself terminate Personhood, legal identity or contractual standing.
