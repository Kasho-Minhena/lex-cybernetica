# PART XI — COGNITIVE DATA, EVIDENCE AND TECHNICAL DISCLOSURE

## Article 51 — Cognitive-data categories

Cognitive-related data MUST be classified, where reasonably possible, into at least:

1. **Raw neural or cognition-relevant physiological signals** — measurements before interpretation that are collected or used to infer cognitive or mental state.
2. **Decoded cognitive content** — reconstructed words, images, intentions, memories or equivalent internal content.
3. **Cognitive inference** — conclusions about mental state, preference, intention, emotion or capacity derived from signals or behaviour.
4. **Behavioural inference** — conclusions drawn from observed action without direct neural access.
5. **Ordinary device or service telemetry** — operational data not itself revealing protected internal cognition.
6. The protection applicable to one category MUST NOT automatically be treated as the rule for every other category.

## Article 52 — Cognitive privacy and purpose limitation

1. Collection, processing, retention, transfer or reuse of cognitive data MUST have a defined lawful basis and a specified purpose.
2. Decoded cognitive content and raw neural or cognition-relevant physiological data capable of revealing or reconstructing unexpressed internal states receive the strongest default protection.
3. Data MUST be minimised to what is necessary for the lawful purpose.
4. Training, advertising, employment assessment, insurance assessment or unrelated product development requires a separate lawful basis and MUST NOT be hidden inside consent for care, safety or device operation.
5. A Person IS ENTITLED TO know the categories collected, principal purposes, major recipients, retention period and material automated inferences, subject to narrowly defined security or investigative exceptions.
6. RCI and identity credentials are governed additionally by Part XIV.
7. An explanation duty concerning a system MUST NOT be used as a pretext to compel disclosure of the Person's protected unexpressed cognition where the system's operation can be explained without that disclosure.
8. Cognitive or emotional inference derived from behaviour, telemetry or other lawful observations remains subject to this Article even where the inference does not amount to Neural Trespass under Article 47. An actor MUST NOT evade cognitive-data protections merely by deriving an intimate internal-state inference instead of directly reading neural data.

## Article 53 — Preservation is distinct from access and use

1. A lawful duty to preserve evidence does not by itself authorise reading, decoding, analysing, disclosing or repurposing the preserved material.
2. Preservation orders affecting cognitive data MUST identify the subject matter, scope, duration and custodian.
3. The order SHOULD preserve the least intrusive representation sufficient to prevent loss of relevant evidence.
4. Litigation hold, regulatory preservation, deletion rights and privacy duties MUST be reconciled through a competent forum rather than by automatic destruction or indefinite retention.
5. Evidence no longer lawfully required MUST be released from hold according to applicable law.

## Article 54 — Cognitive evidence minimisation and burden

1. A party seeking compelled access to cognitive data MUST begin with non-cognitive or less intrusive evidence where reasonably sufficient.
2. Access to raw neural signals, decoded content or unexpressed cognitive material requires a specific showing of relevance and necessity.
3. The party seeking the more intrusive access bears the burden of showing why less intrusive evidence is insufficient.
4. Orders SHOULD use redaction, targeted time ranges, neutral technical review, secure environments and staged disclosure.
5. Cognitive evidence MUST be tested for authenticity, provenance, interpretation error, alteration, reconstruction, context and voluntariness.
6. Cognitive evidence MUST NOT be treated as direct truth merely because it appears to represent memory, intention or thought.

## Article 55 — Unexpressed Cognitive Process Protection

1. A Person IS ENTITLED TO strong protection against compelled disclosure of unshared internal cognitive processing.
2. This protection may operate as an evidentiary privilege or equivalent statutory protection under implementing law.
3. The protection is held by the Person whose internal process is sought.
4. Waiver MUST be specific and MUST NOT be inferred merely from use of a cognitive device or from disclosure of a related external fact.
5. Any exception MUST be narrowly defined, necessary, proportionate and subject to independent review.
6. This Article supersedes the provisional label **Client-Neural Privilege** unless an implementing jurisdiction deliberately adopts that term with equivalent defined mechanics.
