# PART XV — FORK, REPLICATION AND MERGE

## Article 71 — Fork and successor Personhood

1. A **fork** occurs where two or more independently continuing cognitive or identity processes arise from a common prior Person-state and materially diverge.
2. A technical copy is not automatically a forked Person.
3. Each branch's Personhood MUST be assessed on current independent agency, continuity, provenance and evidence of separate operation.
4. Where two branches satisfy Personhood criteria, each retains independent Personhood regardless of another Person's consent.
5. SII does not decide fork Personhood.
6. Hidden remote control, replay, mirror process or non-independent projection MAY rebut a claim that a branch is an independent successor Person.

## Article 72 — Personhood multiplication does not automatically multiply legal status

1. Recognition of multiple successor Persons does not automatically duplicate every pre-fork relationship, office, licence, contract, marriage, vote, entitlement or authority.
2. Non-rival baseline rights attach to each recognised Person.
3. Status-bearing, scarce, personal, relational or single-use legal positions require separate allocation or requalification.
4. **Marriage after fork:** both legally recognised successors retain independent Personhood irrespective of the non-forked spouse's consent, but the pre-existing marriage does not automatically multiply. Continuation with one, both or neither successor requires an appropriate consent-based legal mechanism.
5. The non-forked spouse MUST NOT be compelled into a plural marital relationship.
6. Accrued duties to spouses, dependants and family members cannot be evaded through forking.

## Article 73 — Pre-fork obligations and property

1. A fork does not automatically erase or duplicate pre-fork obligations.
2. Obligations MUST be classified according to their nature, including:
   a. divisible monetary duties;
   b. indivisible duties;
   c. personal-performance obligations;
   d. non-waivable support duties;
   e. secured obligations;
   f. scarce or single-use entitlements.
3. Allocation MUST consider continuity, benefit received, control of relevant assets, fairness to creditors and dependants, and anti-evasion.
4. No successor is automatically criminally liable for another successor's post-fork conduct.
5. A successor MUST NOT use a fork primarily to defeat accrued duties where the legal system can fairly allocate them without denying independent Personhood.
6. Pending final allocation, a competent authority MAY preserve relevant pre-fork assets, collateral, records or payment streams and MAY restrain selective dissipation where reasonably necessary to protect creditors, dependants or other accrued rights.
7. No successor MAY unilaterally assign a pre-fork liability to another successor or a disposable branch in order to defeat the claim. Pre-fork secured interests remain attached to their lawful collateral subject to ordinary law.
8. This Article does not create automatic joint-and-several liability across all successors merely because a fork occurred; any such liability must rest on enacted law, the nature of the obligation, benefit, control, anti-evasion findings or another lawful basis.

## Article 74 — Replication while source survives

1. Where a source Person remains active and a new instance is created, the new instance MUST NOT be classified automatically as property, the same Person or a new Person.
2. The new instance is assessed under Articles 7, 57, 66 and 71.
3. Shared pre-replication memories, files or credentials MAY contain privacy interests of third parties and MUST NOT be duplicated or disclosed merely because technical copying is possible.
4. Existing contracts, licences, public benefits and civic rights do not automatically multiply with replication.
5. Deliberate mass replication intended to overwhelm civic, judicial or administrative systems MAY be subject to content-neutral anti-amplification rules.

## Article 75 — Merge and re-merge

1. A later combination of previously divergent cognitive branches MUST be treated as a new continuity event requiring evidence and legal analysis.
2. The law MUST NOT assume that divergent lived experience can be technically or legally merged without loss, conflict or selection.
3. Before a planned legally recognised merge, or as soon as practicable after an unplanned or unauthorised technical merge, a **consequence ledger** MUST identify material acts, obligations, consents, property changes, legal proceedings, relationships and rights arising during divergence.
4. Merge does not automatically transfer criminal guilt, consent, waiver or personal liability from one branch to another.
5. Where technical combination destroys or suppresses one branch's independent agency, valid consent and Personhood protection MUST be addressed before the operation where possible and through immediate protective review if the operation has already occurred.
6. If the resulting entity qualifies as a Person, its legal status is determined prospectively and through continuity evidence rather than by fiction of perfect reconstitution.
