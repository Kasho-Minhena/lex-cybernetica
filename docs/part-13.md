# PART XIII — DEVELOPING PERSONS, SUPPORT AND HEALTHCARE

## Article 61 — Protected developmental status

1. A developing Person IS ENTITLED TO protection proportionate to actual vulnerability, dependence and decision-specific capacity.
2. Age MAY be used as a protective proxy where an implementing jurisdiction has a legitimate, evidence-based developmental reason for doing so, particularly for biological children, but age alone MUST NOT be mechanically transplanted across different substrates or development paths.
3. Chronological age since synthetic activation, fork or restoration MUST NOT by itself create minority or incapacity where continuity and decision-specific capacity indicate otherwise.
4. A Person who appears highly capable MAY still require targeted support where objective evidence shows a specific developmental vulnerability.
5. A developing Person's views, preferences and participation MUST receive weight appropriate to demonstrated understanding and evolving capacity.
6. Protective authority MUST be reviewable and MUST NOT become permanent by inertia.
7. A newly instantiated entity whose Personhood is unresolved remains governed by Article 8 during the claim period. If and to the extent the entity is recognised as a developing Person, the protections of this Part apply cumulatively rather than replacing Article 8 protections that remain relevant to the unresolved or transitional issue.

## Article 62 — Creator, parent, caregiver and provider roles

1. Creator status, parentage, caregiving, guardianship, infrastructure control and economic ownership are separate roles.
2. A creator does not automatically become a parent.
3. A parent or caregiver does not acquire ownership of the Person or unrestricted access to cognition, communications or identity credentials.
4. A provider controlling indispensable infrastructure MUST NOT use that control to obtain guardianship-like authority without lawful basis.
5. A conflict of interest MUST be disclosed where one actor simultaneously controls support, property, credentials and legal representation.

## Article 63 — Non-waivable duties and accountable coordination

1. Where multiple Persons or entities share responsibility for a developing Person, implementing law MUST identify an accountable coordinator for urgent care and legal administration where fragmentation would otherwise create a protection gap.
2. If no responsible private actor is available or able to coordinate urgently, a competent public authority MUST be able to designate an interim coordinator of last resort without thereby extinguishing accrued private duties or automatically transferring ultimate financial responsibility to the public.
3. Contractual allocation MAY divide roles but MUST NOT waive minimum duties of support, safety, continuity, emergency response or protection from abandonment where such duties have arisen.
4. A contributor MUST NOT escape an accrued non-waivable duty merely by withdrawing from the project, transferring shares, dissolving an affiliate or disclaiming parentage after dependency has arisen.
5. Responsibility MUST be allocated according to causal role, undertaking, control, foreseeability and the developing Person's needs.
6. Coordination authority MUST remain reviewable and separate from property or IP rights.

## Article 64 — Capacity of developing and fresh-successor Persons

1. A newly divergent successor Person who was legally capable immediately before the continuity event IS PRESUMED to retain capacity unless a decision-specific assessment under Part III shows otherwise.
2. A developing Person who did not previously hold adult legal capacity is entitled to participation, support and capacity-sensitive decision-making under Part III and applicable developmental or child-protection law; this Article does not create an automatic presumption of full adult capacity for biological children or other genuinely developing Persons.
3. Short chronological existence alone MUST NOT create legal minority for a synthetic or successor Person.
4. Lack of independent lived experience MAY justify a temporary safeguard only where objectively connected to a high-risk decision.
5. Protective measures MUST prefer support and education over broad substitution of authority.
6. A Person MAY challenge a developmental or guardianship classification at reasonable intervals and on material change.

## Article 65 — Healthcare, genomic and Genesis-related decisions

1. Medical, genetic, gestational, cybernetic or substrate interventions affecting a developing or newly created Person require purpose-specific lawful authority or consent.
2. Decision-makers MUST minimise unnecessary irreversible harm.
3. Where creator, parent, provider, researcher or state interests conflict with the Person's interests, independent representation or review MUST be available.
4. A provider or controller of a technologically mediated Genesis process MUST exercise care proportionate to its control, foreseeable risk and the vulnerability of the developing Person; the Person MUST NOT be treated as a defective product merely because ordinary safety or negligence principles are applied to the provider's conduct.
5. A material later-discovered genetic, epigenetic, cognitive-architecture or provenance alteration that may materially affect the Person's health, identity, reproductive interests or safety MUST be disclosed to the affected Person or lawful representative through a clinically or technically appropriate process, subject to protective handling of third-party data.
6. Heritable or developmental interventions MUST be described according to the evidence actually available; absence of detected error does not equal proof of absence of material genomic or developmental risk.
7. A negative targeted test MUST NOT be represented as comprehensive safety proof where known assay limits leave material categories of risk untested.
8. Future technical claims concerning genome editing, artificial gestation or developmental engineering MUST be assessed under the physical-reality and uncertainty rules in Articles 5 and 98.
